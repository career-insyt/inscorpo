INSYT Flagship Course Architecture
INSYT should build the five flagship courses as short, staged, job-facing pathways rather than long monoliths: 3
stages for Video Editing + AI Production, 4 stages for Advanced Excel, 3 stages for Gamma + Canva + PowerPoint,
3 stages for AI for Work, and 2 layers for GRE, with every stage anchored to authentic tasks, short videos,
embedded practice, spaced retrieval, and employer-readable outputs. The evidence is strongest that completion
improves when courses are tightly structured, practically useful, mobile-friendly, and paired with interactive
exercises and spaced review, while market demand is shifting toward AI, analytics, communication, and digital
content creation rather than generic “tool tutorials” (Lee et al., 2021; Fyfield et al., 2022; Deng & Gao, 2023;
Ogunsakin et al., 2026; Tee et al., 2024; Jaiswal et al., 2021).
Portfolio Architecture
The five-course portfolio breaks into three demand clusters: digital content production, business analytics, and
career-credential preparation. Employer-facing evidence consistently elevates information and data literacy,
problem-solving, communication, collaboration, digital content creation, and continuous learning as high-value
skills, with substantial gaps still reported in graduates and early-career workers (Tee et al., 2024; Weritz, 2022;
Alam et al., 2025; Jaiswal et al., 2021).
The commercially safer portfolio is therefore not five unrelated courses. It is one shared architecture: diagnostic →
short lesson → guided task → independent artifact → timed assessment → public proof. That model fits current
workplace-learning evidence on personalized training paths, skills intelligence, adaptive feedback, and modular
progression (Pham et al., 2026; Tusquellas et al., 2025; Chen, 2022).
INSYT should price for low-friction entry but premium proof. Online learning evidence shows that opaque pricing,
technical instability, and restricted access depress engagement, especially in low-resource settings, while
transparent pricing and flexible models help completion and trust (Ogunsakin et al., 2026; Yu & Tai, 2025; Chen &
Geng, 2021).

Learning Design
The strongest pedagogical pattern is not “more video.” Learners favor organized, practical, comprehensible videos
with short duration, embedded questions, post-video assessments, slides, readings, and cases (Deng & Gao, 2023).
Video design reviews also support shorter, segmented, coherent clips with learner control rather than long
uninterrupted lectures (Fyfield et al., 2022; Navarrete et al., 2023).
INSYT’s reusable lesson template should therefore be: hooked scenario → 3–5 minute concept clip → live
demonstration → guided replication → independent challenge → auto-feedback → retrieval question →
spaced revisit. That sequence aligns with evidence for learning by example, learning by doing, interactive video￾exercise interleaving, and adaptive tutoring (Ou et al., 2019; Çelik et al., 2024).
Spaced repetition should be built into every course rather than reserved for GRE. Across professional and health
education, spaced repetition improves learning, retention, transfer, and even behavior change relative to massed
study (Price et al., 2024; Martinengo et al., 2024; Carpenter et al., 2022).
This also implies a production rule: every stage should end in a performance task, not a quiz-only certificate.
Employers value practical orientation, simulation, and real-world application in micro-credentials far more than
short credentials alone (Bruguera et al., 2024; Maruf et al., 2022).
This timeline tracks how course design evidence shifted toward practice and personalization.
The arc moves from format and psychometrics toward adaptive learning, skills alignment, and work-embedded
assessment. More recent evidence increasingly treats short modular learning as effective only when paired with
active practice, feedback, and labor-market relevance.

Course Blueprints
Video Editing + AI Production should use 3 stages. Stage 1 is CapCut Production Basics: mobile/desktop editing,
pacing, subtitles, hooks, social cuts, and export workflows, because AI tools democratize production but still vary
in quality and control (Logeswari & G, 2024; Ferreira & Campos, 2025). Stage 2 is AI-Assisted Workflow: Gemini
for ideation, scripting, shot lists, prompt drafting, and revision; ElevenLabs for voiceover where naturalness matters,
since ElevenLabs tends to rate closest to human speech among tested systems, though prosody remains a real
limitation (Park & Choo, 2024; Bakkouche et al., 2026; Bakkouche et al., 2025; Ada et al., 2024). Stage 3 is
Advanced AI Cinematography, where Higgsfield belongs conceptually: the justified step beyond
CapCut/Gemini/ElevenLabs is controllable image-to-video, camera trajectory design, shot design, motion control,
and consistency across viewpoint changes (He et al., 2024; Xu et al., 2024; Shi et al., 2024; Xing et al., 2025).
Advanced Excel should use 4 stages, not 2 or 3. The evidence repeatedly separates basic familiarity, intermediate
reporting, advanced analytics, and professional application (Kirsten & Du Toit, 2025; Cruz et al., 2026; Yuningsih et
al., 2024). Stage 1 is spreadsheet foundations and formula fluency. Stage 2 is reporting and analysis with PivotTables
and charts. Stage 3 is Power Query, ETL, and cross-tool workflows, because Power Query specifically maps well to
business-analyst competencies and realistic case tasks (Nazarov & Nazarov, 2020; Maruf et al., 2022). Stage 4 is
business decision support: messy datasets, manager memos, dashboarding, and scenario models (Domino et al.,
2021; Maruf et al., 2022).
Gamma + Canva + PowerPoint should use 3 stages with strict tool boundaries. Gamma is first because it is
optimized for rapid automated deck generation and app-like interactive presentation experiences (García Sánchez,
2025; Anas, 2024). Canva is second for visual polish, branded assets, social/graphic repurposing, and easy slide
aesthetics that raise confidence and engagement (García Sánchez, 2025; Supriadin & Mustaufy, 2025; Hasibuan et
al., 2025; Jamaludin et al., 2025). PowerPoint is final for advanced corporate control: dense business structuring,
custom layouts, executive editing, and environments where export flexibility and standard office expectations
dominate. The key principle is to stop teaching overlap once a tool’s comparative advantage ends (García Sánchez,
2025; Martyshkin et al., 2025).
AI for Work / Career / Business should use 3 stages. Stage 1 is AI Productivity Fundamentals with Gemini as
primary interface, prompt quality, verification, and office tasks, because prompt engineering quality strongly
shapes usefulness (Park & Choo, 2024). Stage 2 is Role Workflows: research synthesis, spreadsheet assistance,
presentation generation, writing, meeting support, and knowledge management (Tusquellas et al., 2024; Chen,
2022). Stage 3 is Human-AI Advantage: decision-making, analytical judgment, communication, and continuous
learning, because basic statistics and routine cognition are more exposed to automation than complex analytical
and interpersonal work (Jaiswal et al., 2021; Caro et al., 2025).

GRE And Assessment
The GRE curriculum should be the least “creative” and the most evidence-constrained. The supplied evidence
supports the current structure as verbal reasoning, quantitative reasoning, and analytical writing, with verbal and
quantitative scored 130–170 and analytical writing scored 0–6 (Davies, 1949; Sturdivant & Relles, 2016). Verbal
question types include text completion, sentence equivalence, and reading comprehension; quantitative includes
quantitative comparison, multiple-choice, data interpretation, and numeric entry (Sturdivant & Relles, 2016; Green
& Wolf, 2011).
The best GRE architecture is 2 layers: Foundation + Timed Mastery. Foundation covers vocabulary-in-context,
reading logic, algebra/arithmetic/geometry/data analysis, and analytical writing structure. Timed Mastery covers
section timing, answer revision strategy, mixed practice, and full simulations. This is because coaching and self￾study effects on the modern GRE are generally modest, test scores are fairly stable on retest, and timing and
response-change strategy matter more than magical hacks (Powers, 1983; Swinton & Powers, 1985; Lee & Wai,
2023; Liu et al., 2015; Bridgeman et al., 2004).
Verbal should receive extra support for international learners. Evidence from Iraq and Pakistan highlights difficult
language, culturally unfamiliar content, sentence equivalence difficulty, and time-consuming reading
comprehension (, 2023; Kalwar, 2021). Analytical writing prep should emphasize structured practice rather than
pre-memorization, since advance prompt familiarity alone showed no performance benefit (Powers, 2005).
GRE also needs honest positioning. Predictive-validity evidence is mixed: some meta-analytic and program-level
studies find positive but often small predictive value, while many reported effects are nonsignificant and STEM
completion findings are especially contested (Kuncel et al., 2001; Feldon et al., 2023; Petersen et al., 2018;
Kurysheva et al., 2024).

Monetization And Market Gaps
Ten cross-course gaps recur. The market overproduces passive tool walkthroughs, long low-completion videos,
generic certificates, shallow AI prompt tips, and redundant feature teaching. It underdelivers on staged
progression, realistic business tasks, adaptive support, role-specific AI use, presentation-to-decision
communication, tool handoff logic, low-bandwidth design, Bangladesh-relevant employability framing, spaced
practice, and portfolio-proof assessment (Ogunsakin et al., 2026; Maruf et al., 2022; Ergunova et al., 2026;
Jamaludin et al., 2025).
Common failures are equally consistent: learners overestimate their starting skill, disengage from synchronous
sessions, abandon when value is delayed, struggle with platform friction, and dislike unclear pricing (Billouard￾Fuentes, 2024; Ogunsakin et al., 2026). Early behavioral data predict completion better than self-report, so INSYT
should trigger interventions from week-one actions, not post-course surveys (Eldemellawy et al., 2026).

INSYT should therefore launch with impulse-priced entry stages, bundle discounts, and artifact-based upsells
